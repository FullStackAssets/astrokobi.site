Imagine a potato farm on the Moon. Researchers at Oregon State University have grown potatoes in simulated lunar regolith that was enriched with organic vermicompost. The plants completed their life cycle and produced yields comparable to Earth‑grown potatoes. This milestone shows that lunar agriculture could be viable for future missions. It also demonstrates that a small amount of organic matter can transform regolith into a productive medium.

<Callout type="takeaway">Potatoes can thrive in lunar regolith if enriched with 30% vermicompost, matching Earth yields.</Callout>

## What happened
Researchers at Oregon State tested five regolith simulants that represent both lunar mare and highland regions. They amended the simulants with vermicompost at 0%, 5–15%, and 30% levels. Plants grown with 30% vermicompost matched the growth, root development, and yield of potatoes cultivated in Earth soil. Those with 5–15% showed partial recovery, while the unamended regolith produced stunted plants with underdeveloped roots. The study, published in npj Microgravity, also confirmed that the potatoes were safe to eat.

## Why it matters
The findings support NASA’s Artemis program, which aims to establish a sustained human presence on the Moon. Producing food locally reduces the need for expensive resupply missions and enhances crew autonomy. Demonstrating that a single crop can complete its life cycle in lunar regolith also provides a proof‑of‑concept for broader in‑situ resource utilization. The work highlights the importance of organic matter as a key ingredient for lunar agriculture.

## How to think about it
When evaluating lunar agriculture, consider the regolith’s physical and chemical properties, the amount of organic amendment needed, and the crop’s life cycle. A framework involves: 1) characterizing regolith simulants; 2) determining optimal vermicompost percentages; 3) assessing crop yield and safety; and 4) scaling the system for long‑term missions.

<Callout type="warning">While 30% vermicompost yields Earth‑like growth in simulants, actual lunar regolith may differ, and scaling to large farms remains untested.</Callout>

NASA Ames spotlighted three outstanding contributors in its November 2026 Stars of the Month program. Engineer Alyssa Villanueva of the LEIA lunar biology team, astronomer Steve Howell for his pioneering telescope instrumentation, and research scientist Samuel LeBlanc for advancing airborne flight‑planning were each honored. Their achievements span space biosciences, astronomical imaging, and Earth‑science mission efficiency, underscoring the center’s multidisciplinary thrust. By publicly recognizing these individuals, NASA Ames reinforces a culture that values technical excellence across its broad portfolio.

<Callout type="takeaway">Recognizing diverse expertise fuels NASA’s multidisciplinary mission success.</Callout>

## What happened
Alyssa Villanueva, an engineer on the Lunar Explorer Instrument for space biology Applications (LEIA) team, earned the Space Biosciences Star for her technical excellence and daily support of engineering validation testing. Steve Howell received the Astronomical Instrumentation Star after a lifetime of leadership on international missions and recent receipt of the Catherine Wolfe Bruce Gold Medal for advancing telescope imaging. Samuel LeBlanc, a research scientist with the Bay Area Environmental Research Institute, was honored for innovative flight‑planning tools that keep NASA’s airborne science missions cost‑effective and scientifically productive.

NASA Ames, founded in 1939 and now home to over 2,300 researchers and a $750 million annual budget, continues to expand beyond its aeronautics roots into astrobiology, small satellites, lunar exploration, and advanced computing. The center’s interdisciplinary environment enables the kind of cross‑domain work highlighted by the Stars of the Month recognitions.

## Why it matters
Highlighting individual contributions showcases the tangible outcomes of NASA’s broad research agenda and signals where future investment may flow. The biosciences work on LEIA informs how living systems respond to lunar conditions, a prerequisite for sustainable lunar habitats. Howell’s instrumentation advances improve data quality for both space‑based and ground‑based observatories, directly benefiting planetary discovery. LeBlanc’s flight‑planning innovations reduce mission costs, allowing more frequent airborne campaigns that support climate and Earth‑science research.

Alperen Şengün burst onto the NBA scene as a versatile big man with a soft touch around the rim. Recent game film, however, shows a noticeable drop in his ability to finish inside the paint. The Rockets have seen fewer second‑chance points and a dip in offensive efficiency when he operates near the basket. This shift matters because the team’s front‑court strategy has long relied on his interior presence.
<Callout type="takeaway">Şengün’s reduced paint production threatens the Rockets’ inside game.</Callout>
## What happened
Over the past month, scouting reports note that Şengün’s post moves have become more tentative and his conversion rate on close‑range attempts has slipped. Defenders are crowding him more aggressively, and he has taken fewer shots within the restricted area.

The Rockets’ offensive sets have adjusted, pulling him out to the mid‑range or perimeter more often, which has not fully compensated for the loss of easy baskets. The team’s overall paint scoring percentage has dipped compared to the same span last season.
## Why it matters
Without a reliable interior scorer, Houston loses a key option for drawing fouls, generating high‑percentage points, and opening up spacing for shooters. Opponents can pack the paint, forcing the Rockets to rely on less efficient jump shots, which could stall their playoff push.

## How to think about it
Fans and analysts should view Şengün’s current struggles as a transitional phase rather than a permanent decline. Consider his age, the adjustments in the Rockets’ offensive scheme, and the potential for skill development. Tracking his shot selection, touch on the glass, and how the coaching staff re‑integrates him into post play will give a clearer picture of his long‑term impact.

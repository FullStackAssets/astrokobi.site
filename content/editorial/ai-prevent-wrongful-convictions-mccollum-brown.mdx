The 2014 exoneration of Henry McCollum and Leon Brown revealed how coerced confessions and ignored evidence can ruin lives. After spending almost 31 years behind bars, the brothers received a $75 million settlement, the largest in U.S. wrongful‑conviction history. Their case illustrates the systemic failures that modern technology could help correct. As artificial intelligence matures, it offers tools to detect bias, verify evidence, and safeguard due process. Understanding this legacy is essential for building fairer justice systems.

<Callout type="takeaway">AI‑driven checks could have prevented the McCollum‑Brown miscarriage of justice.</Callout>

## What happened
Henry McCollum and Leon Brown, teenagers with intellectual disabilities, were coerced into false confessions for a 1983 rape and murder in North Carolina. Despite a lack of physical evidence, they were convicted and sentenced to death and life imprisonment, respectively.

In the early 2010s, DNA testing of crime‑scene material identified a different perpetrator, Roscoe Artis, leading to the brothers' exoneration in 2014. Their release after nearly three decades prompted a $75 million civil award, highlighting the cost of procedural failures.

## Why it matters
The case underscores how human bias, inadequate interrogation practices, and suppressed evidence can produce irreversible harm. AI systems that audit police transcripts, flag inconsistencies, and cross‑reference forensic data could intervene before convictions solidify. Such tools would affect defendants, prosecutors, and the public’s trust in the legal system.

## How to think about it
Adopt a layered review framework: (1) use AI to flag high‑risk cases, (2) require human experts to reassess flagged material, and (3) implement independent oversight to audit AI outputs. This approach balances technological efficiency with ethical safeguards.

<Callout type="warning">Do not replace courtroom judgment with AI verdicts; the technology should augment, not substitute, human decision‑making.</Callout>

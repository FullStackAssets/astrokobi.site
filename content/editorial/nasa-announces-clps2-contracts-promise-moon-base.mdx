NASA’s latest award package, unveiled at the MAX POWER aerospace expo, signals a major push toward a sustained lunar outpost. The agency selected eight commercial firms for the next phase of its Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS‑2) contract, a 10‑year, $10 billion master agreement. Alongside the contracts, NASA introduced the nuclear‑powered rover PROMISE, designed to haul heavy payloads across the Moon’s South Pole. These moves aim to deliver the first elements of a Moon Base before the next crewed landing. The initiative reshapes how industry and government will work together on lunar infrastructure.

<Callout type=\"takeaway\">NASA’s new CLPS‑2 contracts and the PROMISE rover mark a decisive step toward a permanent Moon Base.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced at the MAX POWER expo that eight companies are eligible to compete for future lunar surface delivery missions under the CLPS‑2 framework. The CLPS‑2 contract is a 10‑year, multiple‑award, indefinite‑delivery/indefinite‑quantity master contract with a combined maximum value of $10 billion.

NASA also increased the value of the existing CLPS‑1.0 contract from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion to fund Phase One Moon Base objectives, keeping the original contract active. The awards cover cargo lander development, launch and orbital transfer services, and surface infrastructure.

Among the new technology projects is PROMISE, a nuclear‑powered rover that will provide high‑thrust, long‑duration mobility for transporting science and construction payloads at the lunar South Pole.

## Why it matters
The CLPS‑2 awards create a reliable logistics pipeline, allowing NASA and its partners to deliver habitats, power systems, and scientific instruments on a predictable schedule. By expanding the cargo‑landed capacity, the agency reduces the risk of single‑point failures and accelerates the assembly of a sustainable outpost. The PROMISE rover adds a new class of surface mobility that can operate through the Moon’s long nights, enabling construction and resource‑utilization activities that were previously limited by solar power.

## How to think about it
Stakeholders should view the CLPS‑2 awards as a modular supply chain: treat each lander service as a building block that can be combined with surface assets like PROMISE. Policy makers can prioritize contracts that demonstrate reusable lander designs to lower long‑term costs. Industry participants should align their roadmaps with NASA’s timeline for the first crewed landing, ensuring that payloads are ready to integrate when the contracts are exercised.

<Callout type=\"warning\">Do not assume the nuclear rover will be operational without thorough safety reviews and planetary‑protection compliance.</Callout>

After 17 years of silence, the legendary sci‑fi magazine Starlog is back, debuting its first annual print issue on Nov. 13, 2026. The revival coincides with the publication’s 50th anniversary, marking half a century since its 1976 launch. Fans will find the new issue packed with the classic mix of interviews, retrospectives, and VFX features that defined its golden era. The comeback reflects a growing fatigue with digital‑only media and a renewed appetite for tactile, collectible content. Its timing also highlights how science‑fiction storytelling continues to shape cultural conversations about the future.
<Callout type="takeaway">Starlog’s return shows that premium print can thrive amid digital fatigue when tied to cultural milestones.</Callout>
## What happened
Starlog debuted in 1976, pre‑Star Wars, under editor Terry O’Quinn, and quickly became the go‑to source for sci‑fi news, TV guides, and film analysis. The magazine ran for 33 years, publishing 374 issues before folding in 2009 due to declining subscriptions.
On Nov. 13, 2026, Fangoria Holdings and io9 co‑founder Annalee Newitz announced the first annual edition, Issue #375, featuring cover art by Gabriel Yeganyan. Pre‑orders opened immediately, and the issue celebrates the 50th anniversary with retrospectives on Star Wars, Star Trek, and Alien.
## Why it matters
The revival signals that niche print can still capture dedicated audiences when it leverages nostalgia and a milestone celebration. It offers a tangible archive of genre history at a time when streaming platforms dominate cultural discourse. By re‑entering the market, Starlog may inspire other legacy publications to explore limited‑run, high‑value print models, reshaping how fandoms engage with media analysis.

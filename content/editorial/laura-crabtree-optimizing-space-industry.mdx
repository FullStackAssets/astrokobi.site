On the latest episode of Space News' podcast Space Minds, Laura Crabtree—CEO of mission‑software platform Epsilon3 and former SpaceX astronaut trainer—joined host Mike Gruss to discuss how space companies can reorganize to shave months off testing and certification cycles. She argued that many delays stem from siloed processes and unclear handoffs between engineering, safety, and regulatory teams. By adopting more fluid, cross‑functional structures, firms can accelerate hardware readiness without sacrificing safety. The conversation is especially timely as the industry races to field reusable launch systems and lunar landers. Listeners left with a concrete set of organizational levers to pull.
<Callout type="takeaway">Speeding up testing and certification starts with reorganizing teams, not just building faster hardware.</Callout>
## What happened
Laura Crabtree explained that the traditional aerospace development flow follows a linear V‑model, which creates bottlenecks when hardware, software, and safety reviews wait on one another. She suggested breaking that model into parallel streams, allowing software and hardware validation to proceed concurrently while keeping regulatory checkpoints visible.

She highlighted three practical steps: embed certification engineers early in design sprints, use high‑fidelity digital twins to run virtual flight tests before hardware is built, and foster a “test‑first” culture where every subsystem carries predefined acceptance criteria tied to regulatory milestones.

Crabtree noted that several of Epsilon3’s client programs have reported noticeable reductions in time‑to‑flight after adopting these practices, though she did not disclose specific numbers.
## Why it matters
Accelerating testing and certification directly reduces capital burn and shortens the window between investment and revenue, a critical factor for both commercial launch providers and government contractors. Faster cycles also improve a company’s ability to respond to emerging market demands—such as lunar payload deliveries or satellite constellations—while maintaining the rigorous safety standards that regulators require.

## How to think about it
Adopt a four‑step framework: **Map** the existing certification flow, **Embed** regulators and safety experts in early design sprints, **Simulate** critical tests with digital twins, and **Iterate** by feeding test results back into design before hardware is built. Applying this loop repeatedly creates a feedback‑rich environment that shortens each subsequent cycle.
<Callout type="warning">Do not sacrifice documented safety evidence to meet speed goals; regulators will still require traceable compliance records.</Callout>

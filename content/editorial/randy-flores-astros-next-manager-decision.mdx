Randy Flores, hired as the Astros' general manager just under two weeks ago, announced at the MLB General Managers Meetings that a new field manager will be chosen soon. The club has already dismissed Joe Espada after three seasons and is evaluating four known candidates: former catcher Brad Ausmus, former slugger Albert Pujols, former third baseman Morgan Ensberg, and Rangers bench coach Luis Urueta. Flores expects to return to Houston by the end of the week with a decision, while also finalizing a revamped coaching staff. This rapid hiring process could set the tone for the Astros' roster construction and on‑field strategy heading into the next season.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Astros are poised to appoint a new manager within days, signaling a swift shift in leadership.
</Callout>

## What happened
Flores detailed that owner Jim Crane, senior advisor Jeff Bagwell, and assistant GMs Charles Cook and Gavin Dickey have all participated in the interview process. Interviews have already taken place: Pujols visited Houston on Thursday, while Ausmus originally interviewed for the GM role before the manager discussion emerged.

The organization also confirmed that several coaches will remain—pitching coach Josh Miller, first‑base coach Dave Clark, field coordinator Jason Bell (who later left for the Red Sox), and hitting coach Victor Rodriguez—while a new bench coach and several other positions are still open. Flores described the staffing plan as being at the "whiteboard stage," dependent on the eventual manager’s preferences.

## Why it matters
A new manager will influence everything from lineup construction to player development philosophy, affecting both short‑term performance and long‑term roster flexibility. The Astros’ recent success has hinged on a data‑driven approach; aligning the coaching staff with a manager who embraces that culture could sustain their competitive edge. Conversely, a mismatch could disrupt the continuity that has kept the team in contention.

## How to think about it
Treat the hiring decision as a strategic lever rather than a quick fix. Evaluate each candidate’s track record in player development, in‑game adjustments, and collaboration with analytics departments. Consider how the prospective manager’s philosophy meshes with the existing front‑office blueprint. For fans, temper expectations: even a well‑matched hire needs time to implement systems and earn players’ trust.

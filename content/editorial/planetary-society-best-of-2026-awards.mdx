The Planetary Society has launched its annual Best of 2026 Awards, inviting members and the public to vote on the year’s most significant space science achievements. Categories this year include Best Performance by a Space Science Mission and Best Planetary Society Accomplishment, among others. Voting runs through November 30, with results slated for early December, turning the community’s preferences into a snapshot of the sector’s highlights. Because the awards aggregate expert and enthusiast opinions, they offer a unique barometer of what the space community values as progress.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Best of 2026 Awards reveal which missions and initiatives the space community deems most impactful this year.</Callout>

## What happened
The Planetary Society opened voting for its Best of 2026 Awards, allowing anyone to cast a ballot on categories such as Best Performance by a Space Science Mission and Best Planetary Society Accomplishment. Voting remains open until Nov. 30, and the Society promises to email the results in early December.

Beyond the ballot, the Society encourages participants to share additional favorites in its online member community, a forum where space enthusiasts discuss the latest discoveries, missions, and policy developments. This engagement helps surface lesser‑known achievements and fosters a sense of collective ownership over the narrative of the year’s progress.

## Why it matters
The awards serve as a crowdsourced indicator of which projects capture public imagination and professional respect, influencing future funding priorities and outreach strategies. Highlighting standout missions can amplify their visibility, potentially attracting new partners or donors. Conversely, categories that receive fewer votes may signal gaps in communication or perceived relevance, prompting societies and agencies to adjust their messaging.

## How to think about it
Treat the Best of 2026 results as a pulse check rather than a definitive ranking. Compare the winners with independent metrics such as scientific citations, mission milestones, and budget allocations to gauge alignment. Use the categories as a framework for personal or institutional reflection: which areas did you value most, and why? Incorporate that insight when prioritizing future projects or when communicating the importance of space science to broader audiences.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that award winners automatically represent the most scientifically valuable missions; consider broader evaluation criteria before drawing conclusions.</Callout>

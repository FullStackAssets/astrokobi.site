Metroid Prime 4: Beyond arrived on the Nintendo Switch in 2025 after five years of development at Retro Studios, but the launch left many fans disappointed. The game’s side‑character filler, an underutilized bike, and a lukewarm ending felt more like a relic of the GameCube era than a modern shooter. Critics noted the average reception and the lingering absence of the earlier Prime titles on Switch, fueling calls for a fresh approach. One proposal borrows from Capcom’s recent success: a Resident Evil‑style remake of the original Metroid Prime trilogy.

<Callout type="takeaway">A modern remake could restore the series’ sci‑fi shooter mojo.</Callout>

## What happened
Metroid Prime 4: Beyond launched on the Switch in 2025 after a five‑year development cycle at Retro Studios. The title introduced new side characters, a bike for open‑world traversal, and an ending that many described as unsatisfying, leading to an average critical reception.

The original Metroid Prime trilogy has never been officially brought to the Switch, either as native ports or through Nintendo Online, leaving a generation of players without access to the series’ roots. The recent Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake on Switch 2 demonstrated Nintendo’s willingness to revitalize classic IPs for modern hardware.

Capcom’s Resident Evil remakes—starting with the 2002 overhaul and continuing with the 2019 and 2023 reimaginings—show how a faithful yet updated experience can reignite interest and sales. The proposal suggests applying that same “recapturing the vibes” mindset to the Metroid Prime trilogy, using the Prime 4 engine as a foundation.

## Why it matters
A successful remake would address the franchise’s current identity crisis, giving Nintendo a proven revenue stream while re‑engaging a fanbase that has drifted toward newer titles. By modernizing graphics, controls, and enemy AI, the series could compete with contemporary sci‑fi shooters, strengthening Nintendo’s portfolio beyond its traditional family‑friendly image. Moreover, a well‑executed revival would set a template for how legacy Nintendo properties can be refreshed without sacrificing their core appeal.

## How to think about it
Fans should evaluate a remake not as a replacement for new content but as a bridge that restores confidence in the brand. Developers can use the updated engine to experiment with fresh mechanics while preserving iconic level design. Stakeholders ought to balance nostalgia with innovation, ensuring that any investment also funds original projects that push the series forward.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid assuming a remake will automatically fix deeper design issues that plagued Prime 4.</Callout>

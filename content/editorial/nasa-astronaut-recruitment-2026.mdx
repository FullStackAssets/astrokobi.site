NASA has opened its astronaut application portal for the first time since 2024, inviting U.S. citizens to apply for a new class that will support the agency’s return to the Moon, the construction of a lunar base, and the eventual crewed missions to Mars. The deadline is 11:59 p.m. EST on Thursday, Dec 3, 2026. Selected candidates will begin training in late 2027 and will be expected to master lunar surface operations, long‑duration habitation, and deep‑space navigation. The call comes as NASA prepares to expand its human‑spaceflight architecture beyond low‑Earth orbit, making this recruitment a pivotal step toward the next era of exploration.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s new astronaut class will be the human engine driving lunar‑base construction and the first crewed journeys to Mars.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced that applications for its next astronaut class are now open, with a submission deadline of 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 3, 2026. The agency emphasizes that the new candidates will train for operations on the lunar surface, life‑support work at a future Moon Base, and the systems needed for eventual crewed missions to Mars.

The previous recruitment cycle in 2024 yielded over 8,000 applications, from which ten candidates were selected in 2025 and are currently completing two years of training. Since the 1960s, NASA has selected roughly 370 individuals as astronaut candidates, and the current corps includes 32 active astronauts. Final selections for the 2026 class are expected in late 2027, with training to commence shortly thereafter.

## Why it matters
The new class will directly shape the human component of NASA’s Artemis lunar‑base program and the agency’s long‑term goal of sending crews to Mars. Successful candidates will influence technology development, mission architecture, and public engagement, affecting everything from commercial partnerships to international cooperation. The recruitment also signals a renewed emphasis on sustained deep‑space presence, which could accelerate policy decisions, funding allocations, and the growth of a space‑focused workforce.

## How to think about it
If you are considering applying, start by mapping your existing skills to the core competencies NASA highlights: technical expertise, teamwork, and resilience under pressure. Build a portfolio that demonstrates hands‑on experience with robotics, EVA (extravehicular activity) simulations, or long‑duration isolation studies. Network with current astronauts and program managers to understand the culture and expectations. Finally, treat the application as a long‑term career move—prepare for years of rigorous training and continuous learning.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that meeting the basic eligibility criteria guarantees selection; the process is extremely selective and competitive.</Callout>

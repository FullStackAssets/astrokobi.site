Martian north polar region continues to surprise scientists with its dynamic ice behavior. A new study examined high‑resolution images of 36 craters captured by HiRISE between 2006 and 2025, spanning Mars Years 28 to 38. The researchers found that 32 of the craters displayed bright‑deposit changes that track the planet’s seasonal frost cycle. These observations link surface frost to underlying water‑ice deposits and open a window onto Mars’ climate variability.

<Callout type="takeaway">Seasonal frost causes most polar craters on Mars to expand and contract each year.</Callout>

## What happened
The international team targeted craters within the northern polar ice cap because previous work had focused on broader cap dynamics. Using HiRISE’s sub‑meter resolution, they compared images taken at opposite points of the Martian year to see how bright deposits on crater floors responded to winter and summer conditions.

Across the sample, 32 craters showed clear brightening in winter and darkening in summer, indicating ice accumulation and sublimation on decimeter scales. The remaining four craters showed no detectable change, suggesting either a stable ice layer or surface conditions that mask seasonal signals.

## Why it matters
Seasonal ice movement proves that water‑ice reservoirs on Mars are not static but actively exchange mass with the atmosphere each year. This behavior refines climate models that aim to reconstruct past atmospheric pressure and temperature regimes, and it helps assess the stability of subsurface ice that could support future exploration. Moreover, the pattern of frost‑driven change informs where volatile‑rich deposits may be concentrated, a key factor for both scientific study and in‑situ resource utilization.

## How to think about it
Treat each seasonal frost observation as a data point in a larger climate puzzle rather than a definitive measure of total ice volume. Cross‑reference crater‑scale changes with orbital climate models, radar sounding, and dust‑storm records to build a multi‑layered picture of water‑ice dynamics. When evaluating potential resources, prioritize craters that show consistent winter brightening, as they likely host accessible near‑surface ice.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that unchanged craters lack seasonal ice; they may simply lack sufficient imaging contrast.</Callout>

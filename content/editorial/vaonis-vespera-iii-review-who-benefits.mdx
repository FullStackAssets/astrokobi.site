I spent several weeks testing the Vaonis Vespera III, capturing targets like the Pleiades over multiple nights. The results reveal how far automated optics have come and who can truly leverage that capability.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Vespera III excels for dedicated astrophotographers willing to invest in its premium price.</Callout>

## What happened
The Vaonis Vespera III is a $2,490 smart telescope that combines a 127 mm aperture, built‑in AI alignment, and mosaic imaging technology. Its multi‑night mode stacks exposures over successive evenings, allowing faint nebulae and star clusters to emerge with richer color and detail. During my test period I repeatedly targeted the Pleiades (M45) and the Orion Nebula, letting the system automatically capture and process the data each night.

Image output consistently showed sharp stars and deep‑sky hues that rival small‑aperture astrophotography rigs. Battery life held through several hours of operation, and the companion app handled alignment without manual tweaks. The primary drawback was the start‑up delay; the telescope often took several minutes to cool and calibrate before beginning a session.

## Why it matters
By delivering near‑professional results in a plug‑and‑play package, the Vespera III pushes the smart‑telescope market toward higher performance thresholds, potentially reshaping how hobbyists approach deep‑sky work. However, its price places it out of reach for casual observers, reinforcing a split between affordable entry‑level devices and premium research‑grade tools.

## How to think about it
If you already own a modest telescope and crave deeper images without learning plate solving, the Vespera III may be worth the upgrade. Otherwise, allocate budget to a larger aperture manual scope or a less expensive smart model. Evaluate your goals—whether you prioritize occasional spectacular frames or regular, hands‑off imaging—before committing.

<Callout type="warning">Do not expect instant readiness; plan for a few minutes of warm‑up before each session, and be prepared for the high upfront cost.</Callout>

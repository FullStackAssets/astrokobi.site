The 2025 release of Metroid Prime 4: Beyond left longtime fans feeling let down. After five years of development at Retro Studios, the game arrived with side characters, a bike, and an ending many found unsatisfying. Critics noted the title felt more like a GameCube era experience than a modern Switch showcase. As the franchise’s last major entry, its lukewarm reception has sparked debate about the series’ future. Proposals now point to a full‑scale remake of the original trilogy as a path forward.

<Callout type="takeaway">A Resident Evil‑style remake could restore the series’ relevance and attract new players.</Callout>

## What happened
Metroid Prime 4: Beyond launched on the Nintendo Switch in 2025 after a protracted five‑year development cycle at Retro Studios. While the game finally arrived, many players criticized its reliance on dated design choices, such as a bike for traversal and side characters that added little to the narrative, culminating in an ending that felt unsatisfying.

The original Metroid Prime trilogy, debuting in 2002 on the GameCube, remains highly regarded for its atmospheric sci‑fi shooter gameplay. Yet none of the earlier titles have been remastered for the Switch, and the gap between Prime 3 (2015) and Prime 4 left expectations high. Observers have suggested that Nintendo could follow Capcom’s successful Resident Evil remakes—modernizing graphics and controls while preserving core gameplay—to breathe new life into the Metroid Prime series.

## Why it matters
A well‑executed remake would address the franchise’s current stagnation, re‑engaging a fan base that has grown restless. Modernizing the trilogy could also introduce Metroid Prime to a younger audience unfamiliar with the original releases, expanding Nintendo’s market share in the sci‑fi shooter niche. Moreover, leveraging the existing Prime 4 engine could reduce development risk compared to building a new title from scratch.

## How to think about it
Treat the remake as a platform for iterative improvement rather than a simple graphical upgrade. Prioritize preserving the series’ atmospheric exploration while updating controls, enemy AI, and visual fidelity to meet current standards. Consider releasing the trilogy in phases—remastering one title at a time—to gauge player response and manage development workload.

<Callout type="warning">Avoid rushing the project; a half‑finished remake could damage the franchise’s reputation more than a delayed but polished release.</Callout>

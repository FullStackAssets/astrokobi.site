When a new series of interviews with leading scholars was published, the recurring headline was that religion remains undefined. The discussion highlighted that even widely studied traditions like Buddhism resist a single, all‑encompassing description. Researchers point to the diversity of belief, practice, organization, and existential meaning as sources of the impasse. This lack of consensus matters because legal frameworks, education policy, and inter‑cultural dialogue all rely on a working definition.

<Callout type="takeaway">Religion cannot be captured by a single universal definition; it is a fluid constellation of beliefs, practices, and social structures.</Callout>

## What happened
Scholars surveyed in the interview series repeatedly noted that existing dictionaries and textbooks offer competing lists of criteria—such as belief in the supernatural, communal rituals, moral codes, and institutional structures—yet none accommodates all known traditions. The debate intensifies when examining Buddhism, which is described in some sources as a religion, in others as a philosophy, and in yet others as a way of life. Wikipedia records that Buddhism counts between 320 million and 540 million adherents, representing roughly 4.1 % to 6.7 % of the global population, yet its practices range from monastic meditation to lay ethical conduct, defying a narrow label.

## Why it matters
Without a shared definition, governments struggle to apply religious‑freedom protections, educators grapple with curriculum inclusion, and inter‑faith initiatives risk mischaracterizing partners. The ambiguity also fuels academic disputes that can spill into public policy, influencing everything from tax‑exempt status to immigration law. Recognizing the definitional limits encourages more nuanced, case‑by‑case approaches rather than one‑size‑fits‑all mandates.

## How to think about it
Treat "religion" as a heuristic rather than a fixed category. When evaluating a belief system, ask three questions: (1) Does it provide a worldview that addresses ultimate concerns? (2) Are there recurring communal practices or rituals? (3) Does it involve a recognized authority or tradition that guides adherents. If the answer is mixed, acknowledge the partial fit and consider the context‑specific implications before applying legal or educational labels.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that a single textbook definition will apply across all cultural and legal contexts.</Callout>

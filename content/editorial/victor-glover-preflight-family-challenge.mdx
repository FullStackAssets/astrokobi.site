At the Starmus VIII festival in Tenerife, NASA astronaut Victor Glover surprised the audience by calling the toughest part of his Artemis II preparation a personal conversation, not a technical hurdle. The crewed lunar flyby, which launched on April 1 2026, marked humanity’s first journey beyond low‑Earth orbit since Apollo 17. Glover explained that sitting down with his four adult daughters to discuss the mission’s risks, including the possibility he might not return, was the most daunting task of his career. This candid moment highlights how family dynamics and psychological readiness are as critical to deep‑space missions as engineering.
<Callout type="takeaway">The hardest pre‑flight challenge for an astronaut can be the honest conversation with loved ones about the mission’s life‑or‑death stakes.</Callout>
## What happened
At Starmus VIII on Oct 18, Glover, speaking alongside Chris Hadfield, said the conversation with his daughters was “maybe the greatest challenge of my astronaut career.” He and his wife Dionna had previously prepared their children for his 2020 Crew‑1 flight by touring Kennedy Space Center and planning communications, but by Artemis II all four daughters were adults, requiring a frank discussion about the mission’s dangers. Glover admitted the talk “did not go well” but emphasized it was necessary, underscoring the personal side of preparing for a historic lunar mission.
## Why it matters
The psychological health of astronauts and the support of their families directly affect mission performance, crew cohesion, and public confidence; neglecting these aspects could jeopardize future deep‑space endeavors. Additionally, transparent family communication sets a precedent for how agencies handle the human element of exploration, influencing recruitment, training, and risk management.

## How to think about it
Agencies should embed family‑readiness programs into mission timelines, treating conversations about risk as mandatory briefings; astronauts can use structured dialogue tools, and families benefit from counseling resources to process uncertainty while maintaining mission focus.
<Callout type="warning">Do not let media coverage of personal family talks undermine the privacy and mental health of the crew.</Callout>
